Voxel City: Animated Town

505 stylized voxel prefabs for a whole town that moves, and Voxel Terrain, a brush to sculpt the land under it: hills, beaches, painted roads and lakes, in voxels that match the town. Houses and shops, landmarks with working parts, a fairground with a turning Ferris wheel, a harbor and a steam train, stone stairs and lookouts, detailed ground tiles and 264 facade decorations. Wind, water, smoke and machinery animate in the shader, the stylized water sends waves up every beach, and one line of C# turns day into night.

Ships with two demo scenes: the town from the videos, on an island where every hill, beach and lake was shaped with the brush, with about 2,500 placed prefabs, a fairground reached by stairs and paths, a harbor with floating boats and more than 200 lamps, and a water example on a Unity Terrain island.

Voxel City is a complete stylized town in one pack: cottages, town houses and eight canal-gable row houses, eleven shops, civic buildings and eight landmarks, a fairground with a Ferris wheel, carousel and circus tent, twenty-one trees with the plants, hedges and topiary around them, street lamps, a working street clock, benches, bridges and fountains, market stalls, boats, docks, a steam train with a level crossing, stone stairs and lookouts, plus 26 detailed 4 × 4 m ground tiles and 264 facade decorations. Every prefab is ready to drop in, at real-world scale, with procedurally shaded voxel textures and baked ambient occlusion.

Voxel Terrain. Sculpt your own ground right in the Scene view, in voxels that match the town, starting flat, from an island or from hills. An eight-mode brush raises, lowers, smooths and flattens, adds and removes water and paints ten ground types, and every separate pond you paint becomes a lake with its own water. Protection zones keep a town square or the ground under a building safe from your strokes, objects follow the ground as it moves, grass tufts grow by themselves and far chunks draw coarser meshes. Import heights from a heightmap, a Unity Terrain or meshes, pick cells of 0.25, 0.5 or 1 m and save the ground as ordinary meshes when a scene will not sculpt. The same brush works while the game runs through a small API, on a copy of the terrain's data. Every hill, beach and lake of the demo island was shaped this way.

Motion is the point. Trees and crops sway in the wind, flags, bunting and awnings flap, chimneys smoke, and windmill sails, clock hands, bells, wheels and the carousel turn on real pivots. The Ferris wheel turns with its gondolas hanging level, the lighthouse sweeps its beams across the harbor at night and the village well lowers its pail on command. Nearly all of it runs in the vertex shader, so nothing needs an Animator, and global channels for wind, mechanisms, water, lights and night change the whole town at once.

Stylized water. Add a sea, a lake, a river or a fountain basin from the GameObject menu and set the water level by moving it. One click on Bake The Coast reads your Voxel Terrain or Unity Terrain and colliders and gives the sea waves that run up the beaches and drain back, with foam along quays and rocks; for close views, the Simulation mode simulates the surf around the camera on the GPU. Planar reflections mirror the quays, boats and lit windows, the water reflects the live sky, and boats and buoys ride the waves with Water Float, leaving a wake when they move.

When night falls, windows, lamps, fair bulbs and stained glass light up, and the lamp prefabs carry real lights that fade in with them. The VoxelLine tool draws wood fences, picket fences and strings of bunting along any curve. Characters, NPCs, traffic logic and gameplay scripts are not part of this pack (townsfolk and furnished interiors come as two separate packs, below); the train and the boats in the demo follow simple paths with the included VoxelMover component.

Compatibility

Render pipelines

Universal Render Pipeline: required. The materials use the seven included URP shaders, which is where the animation lives. Turn on the Depth Texture (in the URP Asset or on the camera) for the water, fountains and lighthouse beams; the demo camera does it for you. The Forward+ renderer is recommended for scenes with many lamps.

Compute shaders: the water's floating objects, wakes and simulated surf use compute shaders; on WebGL the sea still draws with its beach waves.

Built-in and HDRP: not supported. The shaders are written for URP, so in another pipeline the materials render pink and nothing moves.

Performance: the animation costs no CPU per object. In big scenes the cost is in real lights and grass: lower the grass density or draw distance, switch off lamp lights you do not need and set the shadow distance in the URP Asset. Planar reflections can be switched off on each water, and on a large Voxel Terrain bigger cells carry the same land with far fewer vertices.

Not included: characters, NPCs, traffic or gameplay scripts, audio, and the presentation sky and effects of the store images.

FAQ

Before you ask

How do I switch the town to night?

Call VoxelCity.SetNight(true, 3f) from any script to fade to night in three seconds, or add the controller from GameObject > VoxelCity > Controller, which also handles the sun, moon, ambient light and fog. In the demo, press N. VoxelCity.SetLights(false, 1f) switches the lamps and windows on their own.

Can I slow down or stop the animations?

Yes, for the whole town at once. VoxelCity.SetAnimationsEnabled(false, 1.5f) freezes everything smoothly, VoxelCity.SetMechanismSpeed(0.5f, 2f) runs windmills, clocks and wheels at half speed, VoxelCity.WindStrength sets the wind and VoxelCity.SetChannel controls each channel (wind, swing, flags, cloth, water, smoke, flicker, mechanisms, lights, night).

A windmill or clock stopped turning around its axis. Why?

The pivot of each moving part is its axis, and static batching merges meshes and loses it. Do not mark animated objects as Batching Static; the prefabs ship without static flags.

How do I draw a curved fence or a string of bunting?

Use GameObject > VoxelCity > Line and pick Wood Fence, Picket Fence or Bunting, then click in the Scene view to add points. The posts, rails, pickets or pennants follow the curve, and marks turn a bay into a gate or an opening. The result is one merged mesh with a collider per bay; Bake To Mesh Asset in the inspector saves it as a normal mesh.

Do the decorations snap to the buildings?

The 264 decorations are ordinary prefabs: drag them onto windows, doors, walls and roofs. Each family has its own model for every spot, and the buildings carry empty VXS_ attach points that mark where each kind fits.

How do I sculpt my own ground?

Use GameObject > VoxelCity > Terrain and pick Voxel Terrain (Flat), (Island) or (Hills), select it and paint in the Scene view. The eight brush modes are in the inspector and in a Scene view overlay, keys 1 to 8 pick them, and each stroke is one undo step. The land is saved with your scene, and an update of the pack never touches it.

Can the ground change while the game runs?

Yes. While the game runs the terrain works on a copy of its data, so the asset never changes. BeginStroke, StrokeTo and EndStroke drive the same brush from code, the colliders catch up when the stroke ends, and VoxelTerrainPlayBrush is a ready example. In the demo scene, the left mouse button sculpts the ground under the pointer.

Can I start from a heightmap or a Unity Terrain?

Yes. Import Heights shapes the terrain from a heightmap (PNG of 8 or 16 bits, or RAW of 16 bits), a Unity Terrain or meshes. If a scene will not sculpt, Save Meshes As Assets writes the ground to one file of ordinary meshes and a prefab.

How do I add water?

Use GameObject > VoxelCity > Water and pick Sea, Lake, River or Fountain Basin, then move it up or down to set the water level. For a sea, press Bake The Coast in the inspector: it reads your Voxel Terrain or Unity Terrain and colliders, and the waves find every beach. On a Voxel Terrain you can also paint water in with the brush: each separate pond becomes a lake of its own. Add the Water Float component to a boat or a buoy to make it ride the waves.

The water or the fountain looks wrong. What is missing?

Usually the Depth Texture. Water, fountains and the lighthouse beams read the scene depth, so turn it on in the URP Asset or on the camera.

Does it work with the Built-in pipeline or HDRP?

No. The look and every animation come from the seven included URP shaders, so the pack needs the Universal Render Pipeline.

Is the sky in the images included?

The demo scene ships with a procedural skybox and its own post-processing. The gradient sky, color grading and light motes in the store images are a presentation setup for the screenshots and are not part of the pack.

Can I use the pack in a commercial game?

Yes. The pack is sold under the standard Unity Asset Store EULA, which allows use in commercial games. It does not allow reselling or redistributing the assets themselves.

Was AI used to make this pack?

Yes, and the store listing will say so too. The voxel models were built by an AI model (Anthropic Claude) that wrote modelling scripts for Blender; no text-to-3D or image-to-3D generator was used. The color palette, the URP and compute shaders, the C# components, the water system and the terrain tool, the Unity prefabs, the demo scenes and the documentation were made by the same process. The publisher defined the town and its catalogue, made the production decisions and decides what is published. The images and videos on this page are renders of the pack in Unity; none was made with an AI image or video generator.
