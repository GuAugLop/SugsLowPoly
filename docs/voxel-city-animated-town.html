Voxel City: Animated Town

560 stylized voxel prefabs for a whole town that moves: houses and shops, landmarks with working parts, a fairground with a turning Ferris wheel, a harbor and a steam train, stone stairs and lookouts, detailed ground tiles and 264 facade decorations. Wind, water, smoke and machinery animate in the shader, and one line of C# turns day into night.

Ships with the demo town from the trailer: about 10,000 placed prefabs, a fairground reached by stairs and paths, and about 390 lamps.

Voxel City is a complete stylized town in one pack: cottages, town houses and eight canal-gable row houses, eleven shops, civic buildings and eight landmarks, a fairground with a Ferris wheel, carousel and circus tent, twenty-one trees with the plants, hedges and topiary around them, street lamps, a working street clock, benches, bridges and fountains, market stalls, boats, docks, a steam train with a level crossing, stone stairs and lookouts, plus 26 detailed 4 × 4 m ground tiles, 264 facade decorations and the 55 terrain and water pieces of the demo world. Every prefab is ready to drop in, at real-world scale, with hand-shaded voxel textures and baked ambient occlusion.

Motion is the point. Trees and crops sway in the wind, flags, bunting and awnings flap, chimneys smoke, boats float, windmill sails, clock hands, wheels, the carousel and the lighthouse lens turn on real pivots, the Ferris wheel turns with its gondolas hanging level, and the water pans and ripples. Nearly all of it runs in the vertex shader, so nothing needs an Animator, and global channels for wind, mechanisms, lights and night change the whole town at once.

When night falls, windows, lamps, fair bulbs and stained glass light up, and the lamp prefabs carry real lights that fade in with them. The VoxelLine tool draws wood fences, picket fences and strings of bunting along any curve. Characters, NPCs, traffic logic and gameplay scripts are not part of the pack; the train and the boats in the demo follow simple paths with the included VoxelMover component.

  • Day to night in one line: VoxelCity.SetNight(true, 3f) fades in lamps, windows and fair bulbs, and the included controller follows with sun, moon, ambient light and fog
  • Shader-driven animation for wind, foliage, crops, swing, cloth, flags, floating, rotation and grass, SRP Batcher compatible
  • Moving parts on real pivots: 58 prefabs have separate sails, hands, bells, wheels, rides or awnings you can drive from code; the Ferris wheel and the village well have their own components
  • Curved fences and bunting: the VoxelLine tool follows any curve, with gates and openings, in one merged mesh
  • 264 facade decorations in 15 families, one model per surface: window boxes, climbers, lanterns, string lights, awnings, wreaths and more
  • 26 detailed ground tiles, 4 × 4 m, for roads, curved paths, plazas, fields, beach and quay, plus 14 modular stone stairs and lookouts
  • 1 unit = 1 meter, Y up, colliders on 286 prefabs (decorations have none)
  • A demo scene with the whole town, a fly camera with adjustable field of view and a README

Compatibility

Render pipelines

Universal Render Pipeline: required. The materials use the six included URP shaders, which is where the animation lives. Turn on the Depth Texture (in the URP Asset or on the camera) for the water, fountains and lighthouse beams; the demo camera does it for you. The Forward+ renderer is recommended for scenes with many lamps.

Built-in and HDRP: not supported. The shaders are written for URP, so in another pipeline the materials render pink and nothing moves.

Performance: the animation costs no CPU per object. In big scenes the cost is in real lights and grass: lower the grass density or draw distance, switch off lamp lights you do not need and set the shadow distance in the URP Asset.

Not included: characters, NPCs, traffic or gameplay scripts, audio, and the presentation sky and effects of the store images.

FAQ

Before you ask

How do I switch the town to night?

Call VoxelCity.SetNight(true, 3f) from any script to fade to night in three seconds, or add the controller from GameObject > VoxelCity > Controller, which also handles the sun, moon, ambient light and fog. In the demo, press N. VoxelCity.SetLights(false, 1f) switches the lamps and windows on their own.

Can I slow down or stop the animations?

Yes, for the whole town at once. VoxelCity.SetAnimationsEnabled(false, 1.5f) freezes everything smoothly, VoxelCity.SetMechanismSpeed(0.5f, 2f) runs windmills, clocks and wheels at half speed, VoxelCity.WindStrength sets the wind and VoxelCity.SetChannel controls each channel (wind, swing, flags, cloth, water, smoke, flicker, mechanisms, lights, night).

A windmill or clock stopped turning around its axis. Why?

The pivot of each moving part is its axis, and static batching merges meshes and loses it. Do not mark animated objects as Batching Static; the prefabs ship without static flags.

How do I draw a curved fence or a string of bunting?

Use GameObject > VoxelCity > Line and pick Wood Fence, Picket Fence or Bunting, then click in the Scene view to add points. The posts, rails, pickets or pennants follow the curve, and marks turn a bay into a gate or an opening. The result is one merged mesh with a collider per bay; Bake To Mesh Asset in the inspector saves it as a normal mesh.

Do the decorations snap to the buildings?

The 264 decorations are ordinary prefabs: drag them onto windows, doors, walls and roofs. Each family has its own model for every spot, and the buildings carry empty VXS_ attach points that mark where each kind fits.

The water or the fountain looks wrong. What is missing?

Usually the Depth Texture. Water, fountains and the lighthouse beams read the scene depth, so turn it on in the URP Asset or on the camera.

Does it work with the Built-in pipeline or HDRP?

No. The look and every animation come from the six included URP shaders, so the pack needs the Universal Render Pipeline.

Is the sky in the images included?

The demo scene ships with a procedural skybox and its own post-processing. The gradient sky, color grading and light motes in the store images are a presentation setup for the screenshots and are not part of the pack.

Can I use the pack in a commercial game?

Yes. The pack is sold under the standard Unity Asset Store EULA, which allows use in commercial games. It does not allow reselling or redistributing the assets themselves.

Was AI used to make this pack?

Yes, and the store listing will say so too. The voxel models were built by an AI model (Anthropic Claude) that wrote modelling scripts for Blender; no text-to-3D or image-to-3D generator was used. The color palette, the six URP shaders, the C# components, the Unity prefabs, the demo scene and the documentation were made by the same process. The publisher defined the town and its catalogue, made the production decisions and decides what is published. The store images and the trailer are real-time Unity recordings of the pack's own prefabs; none was made with an AI image or video generator.
